\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented Sa2VA-Select, a compatible extension of Sa2VA for studying learnable visual token selection in unified dense grounded image and video understanding.
Unlike VQA-style compression settings, Sa2VA-Select must preserve visual evidence for both language generation and segmentation-sensitive \texttt{[SEG]} token prediction.
By applying a learnable selector to the fused image/video+VP stream before the MLLM, and by using differentiable training-time selection with inference-time hard Top-$k$ pruning, our method provides a controllable way to trade visual token budget for efficiency.
Across image/video segmentation, multimodal conversation, qualitative analysis, ablation study, and efficiency profiling, our results support a consistent conclusion: learnable token selection remains effective after being transferred to a unified dense grounded model, provided that the selector is integrated compatibly with Sa2VA's unified visual stream.
In particular, the current experiments identify a favorable operating region around 30--40\% retention, where Sa2VA-Select preserves the strongest overall dense grounding performance while already achieving meaningful efficiency gains.
The qualitative analysis further suggests that the selector improves robustness to distractor-dominant confusion and temporal drift, while the ablation results indicate that the train-time/inference-time compatibility introduced by curriculum-based constraint matching is important for turning soft selection into effective hard pruning.
Taken together, these findings position Sa2VA-Select as an empirical step toward efficient unified dense grounding rather than as a standalone compression trick for QA-style MLLMs.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
This study is an initial step toward efficient unified dense grounding, and several aspects remain to be strengthened.
First, the current evaluation focuses on Sa2VA-1B and a limited set of retention ratios; larger backbones and more diverse budgets should be evaluated in future work.
Second, although we observe favorable trade-offs, a more detailed analysis of selected versus discarded tokens is still needed to explain more precisely what spatial, temporal, and prompt-conditioned evidence the selector preserves.
Third, the current profiling focuses on memory, prefill time, and end-to-end latency under a fixed evaluation setting; broader system-level profiling would clarify deployment behavior under different decoding policies, video lengths, and hardware regimes.
Finally, it would be valuable to test whether the same compatible integration strategy transfers to stronger Sa2VA variants and to more domain-specific high-resolution scenarios, such as remote sensing or medical imagery, where the tolerance to token removal may differ substantially from generic benchmarks.
