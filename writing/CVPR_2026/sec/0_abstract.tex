\begin{abstract}
We study visual token selection for \emph{dense grounded} image and video understanding, where models must jointly support instruction-following conversation and pixel-level outputs under long spatiotemporal contexts.
We propose \textbf{Sa2VA-Select}, which learns to retain visual features that best support the generation of \texttt{[SEG]} tokens---intermediate segmentability cues understood by SAM2---while reducing the visual token budget.
Our selector is instantiated with (i) a lightweight importance scorer (LIS), (ii) a train-time differentiable budgeted gate (DiffTopK) that aligns with inference-time hard Top-$k$, and (iii) a curriculum (CAS) that stabilizes multi-task optimization under progressively tighter budgets.
For compatibility with Sa2VA, selection is applied on the unified image/video+VP stream \emph{after} fusion and \emph{before} the multimodal LLM, without changing the baseline task interface.
At inference, we switch to standard hard Top-$k$ pruning to obtain real compute/memory savings.

On Sa2VA-1B, our model improves the overall multi-task average to 102.9\% of the baseline while retaining only the top 30\% visual tokens, with notable gains on referring video segmentation (MeViS: 41.7$\rightarrow$47.6; ReVOS: 47.6$\rightarrow$51.3).
On the Ref-SAV benchmark, our 1B variant reaches 57.6 J\&F, exceeding both Sa2VA-8B (50.0) and zero-shot Sa2VA-8B (41.3) in our evaluation.
We further observe reduced peak GPU memory (2.76$\rightarrow$2.46 GB) and faster prefill (40.16$\rightarrow$35.14 ms) at 30\% retention, demonstrating a favorable accuracy--efficiency trade-off for dense grounded multimodal understanding.
\end{abstract}
