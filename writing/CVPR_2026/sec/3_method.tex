\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We present Sa2VA-Select, a compatible extension of Sa2VA that applies learnable visual token selection to the unified visual stream before the multimodal LLM.
Our goal is not to redesign Sa2VA's task interface, but to reduce the visual token budget while preserving the evidence most useful for generating accurate \texttt{[SEG]} tokens and maintaining the model's joint segmentation-and-conversation capability.
To this end, we instantiate the selector with three components adopted in a Sa2VA-compatible manner: a Unified Visual Token-oriented Learnable Importance Scorer (UVT-LIS), a train-time differentiable Top-$k$ relaxation (DiffTopK), and a curriculum annealing strategy (CAS) that narrows the gap between soft training-time selection and hard inference-time pruning.

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}
Sa2VA-Select targets \emph{unified dense grounded understanding}, where the same model must support both pixel-level prediction and language generation over images and videos.
In Sa2VA, visual inputs and visual prompts are converted into a unified visual stream that is consumed by the MLLM, and the MLLM produces \texttt{[SEG]} tokens that act as segmentability cues for SAM2.
This makes the pre-LLM visual stream a natural intervention point: it already contains the fused evidence needed by downstream decoding, yet it is also the source of a substantial compute and memory burden.

Following the baseline abstraction, for an instance $i$ with input text tokens $T_i$, image/video input (denoted as $I_i$ or $V_i$), and additional visual prompt tokens $VP_i$, the model outputs answer tokens $T_o$ and optionally masks (or masklets) $M_o$:
\begin{equation}
T_o,\ M_o = \mathrm{LLM}(\{I_i, V_i, VP_i\},\ T_i).
\label{eq:sa2va_unified_task}
\end{equation}
Here $V_i$ denotes the raw video input, while bold symbols (e.g., $\mathbf{V}$) denote token features.
For chat-only tasks, $M_o$ is absent; for referring segmentation tasks, $M_o$ is an image mask or a spatio-temporal masklet.

Our design inserts a budgeted selector between Sa2VA's fused visual representation and the MLLM.
During training, the selector uses a differentiable relaxation so that downstream supervision can update token importance scores end-to-end.
During inference, it switches to standard hard Top-$k$ pruning so that the reduced token count corresponds to actual efficiency gains.
Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the full training and inference pipeline. In particular, the figure highlights that the selector operates on Sa2VA's unified visual tokens, uses soft selection together with a hard-mask target during training, and switches to hard selection at inference for actual token pruning.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Let $\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ denote the unified visual token features required by the LLM, where $N$ is the number of tokens and $D$ is the feature dimension.
Given a retention ratio $\rho\in(0,1]$, we define the retained token budget as
\begin{equation}
k = \lfloor \rho N \rfloor.
\label{eq:method_token_budget}
\end{equation}
Our objective is to keep only $k$ visual tokens while preserving the features most helpful for downstream dense grounding and conversation.

\paragraph{Unified visual feature stream (Sa2VA baseline).}
Sa2VA converts visual prompts into a binary mask $\mathbf{M}\in\{0,1\}^{H\times W}$ and forms VP-conditioned tokens by masking a downsampled thumbnail:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{\mathcal{I}} &= \mathrm{Down}(\mathcal{I}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\bar{H}\times\bar{W}\times 3},\\
\bar{\mathbf{M}} &= \mathrm{Down}(\mathbf{M}) \in \{0,1\}^{\bar{H}\times\bar{W}},\\
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{vp}} &= \bar{\mathcal{I}} \odot \bar{\mathbf{M}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:method_vp_masked_thumb}
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes broadcasting element-wise multiplication.

The masked thumbnail is then encoded and appended to the original Sa2VA visual tokens:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{vis}} &= \mathrm{Enc}(\mathcal{I}\ \text{or}\ \mathcal{V}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\mathrm{vis}}\times d},\\
\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{vp}} &= \mathrm{Enc}_{\mathrm{vp}}(\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{vp}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\mathrm{vp}}\times d},\\
\mathbf{X} &= [\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{vis}};\ \mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{vp}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{(T_{\mathrm{vis}}+T_{\mathrm{vp}})\times d}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:method_unified_tokens}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{vis}}$ includes full image/video tokens and thumbnail tokens, while $\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ encodes the VP-masked thumbnail.
For clarity, we denote this fused stream as $\mathbf{V}$ when describing token selection.
Applying selection at this stage allows the selector to jointly rank ordinary visual evidence and VP-conditioned evidence under a shared budget.

\subsection{Unified visual token-oriented learnable importance scoring (UVT-LIS)}
\label{sec:method_lis}
Given the fused visual tokens $\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$, UVT-LIS predicts an importance score $s_i$ for each token $i$.
In our setting, these scores should reflect how useful each token is for preserving dense grounding quality, especially the generation of reliable \texttt{[SEG]} cues, while still supporting the model's language output.
UVT-LIS follows the simplified self-attention-style scorer used in VisionSelector, but applies it to Sa2VA's unified visual token stream. Concretely, we compute
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{V}\mathbf{W}_{q},\quad \mathbf{K}=\mathbf{V}\mathbf{W}_{k},
\label{eq:method_lis_qk}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_{q},\mathbf{W}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times d}$ are learnable projections and $d$ is the hidden dimension.
We then form a score matrix and average interactions to obtain per-token importance:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{A}=\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}},\quad s_i=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{ij}.
\label{eq:method_lis_score}
\end{equation}
This scorer is lightweight, easy to insert into Sa2VA, and provides a token ranking over unified visual tokens that can be optimized under downstream supervision.

\subsection{Train-time differentiable selection via DiffTopK}
\label{sec:method_difftopk}
The target inference operator is hard Top-$k$ pruning, but a discrete Top-$k$ cannot be optimized directly with standard backpropagation.
We therefore use a differentiable relaxation during training and reserve hard pruning for inference.
Specifically, DiffTopK produces a soft selection mask $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{soft}}\in(0,1)^N$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{soft}} = \mathrm{DiffTopK}(\mathbf{s}) = \sigma(\mathbf{s}+t),\quad \sum_{i=1}^{N} M_i \approx k,
\label{eq:method_difftopk_forward}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function and $t$ is a scalar threshold solved by bisection in the forward pass to satisfy the budget.
The resulting mask preserves the ranking induced by the scores:
\begin{equation}
[\mathrm{DiffTopK}(\mathbf{s})]_i > [\mathrm{DiffTopK}(\mathbf{s})]_j \Leftrightarrow s_i > s_j.
\label{eq:method_difftopk_mono}
\end{equation}
We apply the mask by scaling token magnitudes,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{pruned}} = \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{soft}} \odot \mathbf{V},
\label{eq:method_pruned_tokens}
\end{equation}
which preserves gradient flow from downstream objectives to UVT-LIS.
In practice, this relaxation lets the model learn token importance from the same multi-task supervision that drives Sa2VA itself.

\paragraph{Backward pass via implicit differentiation.}
We differentiate $\mathbf{M}=\sigma(\mathbf{s}+t)$ under the implicit constraint $\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma(s_i+t)=k$.
Let $\mathbf{v}=\sigma'(\mathbf{s}+t)$ with $v_i=M_i(1-M_i)$.
From the constraint, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_j}=-\frac{v_j}{\sum_i v_i}.
\label{eq:method_implicit_dt}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian of $\mathbf{M}$ with respect to $\mathbf{s}$ can be written as
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{M}}{\partial \mathbf{s}}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{v})-\frac{\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\top}}{\sum_i v_i}.
\label{eq:method_implicit_jac}
\end{equation}
Therefore, for an upstream gradient $\mathbf{g}=\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{M}}$, the gradient with respect to scores is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{s}} = \mathbf{v}\odot\mathbf{g} - \frac{\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{g}}{\sum_i v_i}\,\mathbf{v}.
\label{eq:method_implicit_grad}
\end{equation}
We include this derivation for completeness because it clarifies how the budgeted soft mask remains trainable despite the implicit threshold.

\subsection{Inference-time hard Top-$k$ pruning}
\label{sec:method_topk_infer}
At inference, we replace the soft relaxation with standard hard Top-$k$ selection to obtain a binary mask $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{hard}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{hard}}=\mathrm{TopK}(\mathbf{s}),
\label{eq:method_hard_topk}
\end{equation}
and physically remove low-score tokens before they enter the MLLM.
This step is what yields actual memory and latency reductions at test time.
We evaluate multiple retention ratios (e.g., 40\%/30\%/20\%/10\%) to quantify how token budget affects both task performance and efficiency, while keeping Sa2VA's downstream interface in \eqref{eq:sa2va_unified_task} unchanged.

\subsection{Curriculum annealing strategy (CAS) for multi-task training}
\label{sec:method_cas}
A remaining challenge is the mismatch between soft selection during training and hard pruning during inference.
To reduce this gap, we adopt a curriculum-weighted objective that gradually encourages the learned soft mask to behave more like the corresponding hard mask.
The total loss is written as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}} + \lambda_t\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{constraint}},
\label{eq:method_total_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}$ aggregates the downstream supervision used by Sa2VA and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{constraint}}$ regularizes the selection process. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}, this objective couples the selector with both QA and segmentation supervision while encouraging agreement between soft and hard selection.

We define the constraint by encouraging the soft mask $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{soft}}$ to approximate the hard Top-$k$ mask $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{hard}}$ through binary cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{hard}} = \mathrm{TopK}(\mathbf{s}),\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{constraint}} = \mathrm{BCE}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{soft}},\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{hard}}).
\label{eq:method_constraint_bce}
\end{equation}
This encourages the soft mask to become more polarized over training, so that the eventual switch to hard pruning causes less mismatch.

The weighting coefficient $\lambda_t$ follows a curriculum annealing schedule.
Early in training, we keep $\lambda_t$ small so that optimization is dominated by downstream task learning; later, we gradually increase its value so that the model is increasingly optimized under a sharper selection constraint:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_t = \lambda_{\mathrm{start}} + (\lambda_{\mathrm{end}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{start}})\times \min\Big(\frac{t_{\mathrm{current}}}{t_{\mathrm{total}}},1.0\Big).
\label{eq:method_cas_schedule}
\end{equation}
This schedule is especially useful in our setting because segmentation quality and language quality may respond differently to aggressive token reduction.

\subsection{Positional encoding (implementation detail)}
\label{sec:method_rope}
We keep the base MLLM's RoPE unchanged.
During inference, hard Top-$k$ pruning removes low-score visual tokens and the remaining tokens are re-packed into a shorter sequence; positional indices are therefore generated on the compressed sequence by the base model, and we do not provide custom position IDs.
This choice is orthogonal to the selector itself and is reported only as an implementation detail.
