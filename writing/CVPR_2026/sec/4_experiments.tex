\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate our Sa2VA-Dev token selection on dense grounded understanding benchmarks spanning image referring segmentation, video referring segmentation, and multimodal conversation.
Unless otherwise specified, results are reported for the Sa2VA-1B baseline and our Sa2VA-1B (ours) variant under different visual token retention ratios.

\paragraph{Evaluation scope and draft status.}
We evaluate (i) dense grounded accuracy under multiple retention ratios, (ii) matched-budget baselines and ablations that isolate the effect of the VP-aware fusion point and train--test alignment, and (iii) efficiency metrics under a fixed decoding protocol to quantify actual savings.
To keep the draft fully compilable, some tables/figures are included as placeholders and are explicitly marked as \emph{planned}; we do not base any claims on these placeholders, and we separate measured results from hypotheses.

\subsection{Evaluation setup}
\paragraph{Retention ratios.}
We test multiple retention ratios $\rho\in\{0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1\}$, corresponding to keeping the top 40\%/30\%/20\%/10\% tokens ranked by LIS at inference.

\paragraph{Tasks and metrics.}
For image referring segmentation, we report cIoU on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg.
For video referring segmentation, we report J\&F on MeViS and Ref-SAV; we also include Ref-DAVIS17 when available.
For multimodal conversation, we follow standard benchmark protocols (e.g., MME/MMBench/SEED-Bench and their video counterparts).

\paragraph{Training datasets and finetuning scope.}
Table~\ref{tab:train_datasets_seg} summarizes the training datasets used for Sa2VA-Dev finetuning.
We initialize from a Sa2VA-pretrained checkpoint and finetune only (i) LoRA parameters and (ii) the learnable attention parameters in LIS (e.g., $\mathbf{W}_q,\mathbf{W}_k$ in \eqref{eq:method_lis_qk}), while keeping the remaining backbone parameters frozen.
We focus on segmentation-related data sources and omit QA-only datasets.

\subsection{Efficiency analysis}
We report peak GPU memory, prefill time, and end-to-end latency in Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}.
At 30\% retention, Sa2VA-1B (ours) reduces peak memory from 2.76 GB to 2.46 GB and improves prefill time from 40.16 ms to 35.14 ms.
We note that end-to-end latency depends on additional factors (e.g., decoding length and system-level overhead) and is not strictly monotonic in our current measurements.

With a fixed decoding length, we expect prefill cost and peak memory to decrease monotonically as $\rho$ decreases, since the attention and KV-cache sizes scale with the number of visual tokens.
We recommend also reporting (i) FLOPs or attention complexity proxies and (ii) tokens-per-second throughput.
