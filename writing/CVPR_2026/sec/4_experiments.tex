\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate Sa2VA-Dev on dense grounded understanding benchmarks spanning image referring segmentation, video referring segmentation, and multimodal conversation.
Unless otherwise specified, we compare the Sa2VA-1B baseline with Sa2VA-1B (ours) under multiple visual token retention ratios.

\subsection{Evaluation setup}
\paragraph{Retention ratios.}
We test multiple retention ratios $\rho\in\{0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1\}$, corresponding to keeping the top 40\%/30\%/20\%/10\% tokens ranked by LIS at inference.

\paragraph{Tasks and metrics.}
For image referring segmentation, we report cIoU on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg.
For video referring segmentation, we report J\&F on MeViS and Ref-SAV; we also include Ref-DAVIS17 when available.
For multimodal conversation, we follow standard benchmark protocols (e.g., MME/MMBench/SEED-Bench and their video counterparts).

\paragraph{Training datasets and finetuning scope.}
Table~\ref{tab:train_datasets_seg} summarizes the training datasets used for Sa2VA-Dev finetuning.
We initialize from a Sa2VA-pretrained checkpoint and finetune only (i) LoRA parameters and (ii) the learnable attention parameters in LIS (e.g., $\mathbf{W}_q,\mathbf{W}_k$ in \eqref{eq:method_lis_qk}), while keeping the remaining backbone parameters frozen.
We focus on segmentation-related data sources and omit QA-only datasets.

\paragraph{Dataset sampling and repeats.}
We use a concatenated dataset with repeat factors for each source: RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg use repeats 5/5/4, and ReVOS/MeViS/Ref-YTVOS/Ref-SAV use repeats 10/4/4/4, respectively. We group batches by modality length to reduce padding variance.

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
Our backbone is InternVL2.5-1B initialized from the Sa2VA-1B checkpoint; the LLM and visual encoder are frozen. We train LoRA with $r=128$, $\alpha=256$, dropout $0.05$, and a lightweight TransformerScorer (in\_features=896) for LIS. The training budget is set to 0.3 (retain 30\% visual tokens); budgets are adjustable for other retention ratios at inference (e.g., 0.1/0.2/0.3/0.4).
We train for 1 epoch with per-device batch size 2 and gradient accumulation 4 on 2$\times$A100-SXM4-40GB (about 20 hours). Optimization uses AdamW with lr $4\times10^{-5}$, betas $(0.9,0.999)$, weight decay 0.05, and gradient clipping at 1.0. We use bfloat16 AMP with dynamic loss scaling and 8 dataloader workers.
The learning-rate schedule applies 5\% linear warmup (start factor $10^{-5}$) followed by cosine annealing to zero. The tokenizer is loaded from the backbone with the \texttt{qwen\_chat} template, max length 8192, right padding, and special tokens [SEG], \texttt{<p>}, \texttt{</p>}, \texttt{<vp>}, \texttt{</vp>}. Images are resized with a 1024 target length. Loss weights are 2.0 for the mask loss and 0.5 for the Dice loss.
For CAS, we use the default linear schedule with $\lambda_{\mathrm{start}}=0.1$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{end}}=3.0$ over training steps. DiffTopK uses the default settings without additional hyperparameters.

\subsection{Main results across tasks}
We follow the original Sa2VA evaluation protocol without modification and present the main results in Table~\ref{tab:overall_table6_summary} and Table~\ref{tab:image_level_bench_sa2va1b_dev}.

\paragraph{Comparison with strong baselines.}
Table~\ref{tab:overall_table6_summary} provides a multi-task summary across image/video segmentation and chat benchmarks. With 40\% visual tokens retained (60\% reduction), Sa2VA-1B (ours) shows substantial gains on video referring segmentation (MeViS: 41.7$\rightarrow$52.2; ReVOS: 47.6$\rightarrow$51.2) while maintaining comparable image referring segmentation (RefCOCOg: 72.3$\rightarrow$74.8). Chat benchmarks remain within a narrow range of the baseline, indicating that token reduction does not collapse general vision-language reasoning.

\paragraph{Detailed results and retention sweep.}
Table~\ref{tab:image_level_bench_sa2va1b_dev} details performance across image-level benchmarks with relative percentages w.r.t. Sa2VA-1B (100\%). At 30--40\% retention, most metrics stay close to the baseline (e.g., MMBench $\approx$96--98\%, SEED $\approx$105--107\%, RefCOCOg $\approx$103\%), while more aggressive pruning (10\%) shows clearer degradation on general QA benchmarks. Overall, 40\% retention provides the best multi-task balance and is selected as the main result point.

\subsection{Ref-SAV validation results}
\label{sec:exp_refsav}

Table~\ref{tab:refsav_dev} compares Dev-1B (our model) at different retention ratios $\rho$ with Sa2VA-8B (zs) and Sa2VA-8B reported in the Sa2VA paper. \subsection{Efficiency analysis}
We evaluate efficiency on MeViS (val\_u), consistent with the baseline Sa2VA protocol, using all samples and report the dataset average on a single A100-SXM4-40GB GPU with the same input processing as Sa2VA-1B. We measure three metrics: max GPU memory, prefill time, and end-to-end (E2E) latency. Prefill time is measured from the start of \texttt{generate} to the first full-sequence forward (seq length $\neq 1$), and E2E latency measures the full \texttt{generate} call (prefill + decoding); peak memory is the maximum allocated GPU memory during generation. We do not use warmup or discard initial samples. Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_mevis} summarizes the trade-offs across retention ratios, while ``Perf.'' is the average of Image Seg and Video Seg relative to Sa2VA-1B (100\%). Lower retention consistently reduces memory and prefill time, with the smallest memory footprint at 10\% retention (2.37~GB) and the fastest prefill at 10\% (34.78~ms). The lowest E2E latency is achieved at 20\% retention (204.96~ms), slightly below the Sa2VA-1B baseline (216.12~ms), while 30--40\% retention increases E2E latency despite higher accuracy on video referring segmentation. Minor performance gains under token reduction should be interpreted cautiously, as they may arise from removing redundant visual tokens rather than systematic improvements.
